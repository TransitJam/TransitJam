### 📊 Recent Policy Briefings & Urban Analytics

#### 🛑 Hong Kong Pedestrian Fatalities: The Data Vindicates the Walkers (October 2026)
Our analysis of 2023–2025 traffic datasets shared in [LegCo](https://gia.info.gov.hk/general/202610/28/P2026102800317_557444_1_1793168507684.pdf) today reveals a stark reality: **76.9% of pedestrians killed in traffic incidents bore zero blame for the crashes which killed them.** 

| Contributory Factor (2025 Data) | Killed | Seriously Injured | Slightly Injured | Total KSI |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **No pedestrian factor recorded** | **40 (76.9%)** | **140 (78.2%)** | **1,779 (77.2%)** | **180** |
| Crossing heedless (at crossing) | 5 (9.6%) | 12 (6.7%) | 89 (3.9%) | 17 |
| Crossing heedless (elsewhere) | 5 (9.6%) | 11 (6.1%) | 190 (8.2%) | 16 |
| In road, not crossing (jaywalking) | 1 (1.9%) | 3 (1.7%) | 46 (2.0%) | 4 |
| Pedestrian inattentiveness | 1 (1.9%) | 13 (7.3%) | 173 (7.5%) | 14 |

*Key Takeaway:* Public enforcement campaigns frequently target jaywalking penalties, yet the data proves "jaywalking" accounts for less than **2% of actual fatalities**. Yes, careless crossing accounts for 20% of deaths, but vehicle speed and driver attentiveness are also often contributory factors here. Advocacy must pivot sharply toward mitigating driver distraction and reducing speed boundaries in dense commercial clusters rather than targetting pedestrians with jaywalking enforcement.
